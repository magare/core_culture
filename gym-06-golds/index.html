The heritage

Same standard.

No rebrands, no gimmicks, no machine that does the lifting for you. The district changed around us; the standard didn’t. Walk the wall.

1998

The garage years

One squat rack, two bench shifts, sixteen members and a kettlebell with a split handle. The rule that still runs the place was written on the wall in marker: the bar doesn’t care who you are — it only counts what you put on it.

16 members · 1 rack · 0 excuses

Iron lands on Foundry Row

The garage outgrew the garage. We took the old foundry workshop on Foundry Row, kept the concrete floors on purpose, and welded the first rig ourselves. Members numbered their own plates — most of those plates are still in service.

The Foundry Yard opens its doors

The coaching charter

We wrote down what the good years had taught us: nobody trains their first year unsupervised, every programme is coached, and a PR gets the same whistle as a first warm-up. The charter hangs by the desk at all three clubs now.

Every session coached — no exceptions

Doors before dawn

The 05:30 shift started as a favour to two dockworkers and became the club’s heartbeat. First light, empty floor, chalk in the air. If you know, you know — and half the district knows.

05:30 — the original members’ hour

Three clubs, one book

Ironworks opened in the Canal Quarter, Southpaw took the old depot end. Same charter, same coaching standard, same plates counted by hand. One membership walks through all three doors — that was the whole point.

One membership · every floor

The standard travels

Twenty-eight years in, the racks are busier and the promise hasn’t moved. The weight on the bar is still honest, the coaching is still earned, and the floor still does the talking. Wherever the next door opens, the standard goes with it.
