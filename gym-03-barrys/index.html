The method

One hour.
Two halves.

Every Crimson Circuit class splits down the middle: half the room starts on the lane, half under the rack — then you swap. You leave having done both, which is why you only need to come back.

Half oneThe lane

Twenty metres of turf, one heavy sled, and your own pace on the clock. Sprints, pushes and carries in coached intervals — the cardio half of the hour, no treadmill in sight and no lap you can quietly coast.

Book the lane

Half twoThe rack

Bars, plates and positions a coach has set for your body, not the next one along. Slow the rep, add the plate, log the number — the strength half of the hour that makes next week's lane faster.

Book the rack

Coaches & community

Coached like
you belong.

“You don't have to be fit to walk in. Walking in is the workout.”

— R. Okafor, Crimson Circuit coach since day one

Every body in the room gets a coach's eyes. First-timers are scaled in from rep one; regulars get the next plate before they ask. Modifications exist for every lane sprint and every rack lift — injury, pregnancy, first month or fiftieth year, the hour bends to you, not the other way round.

The room stays small on purpose: two coaches to sixteen, names learned by week two, and a bench saved when you book. That's the culture part.
